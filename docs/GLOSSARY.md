# Glossary (plain language)

- **Runner** — the hacker player. Tries to break into the Corp's servers and steal agendas.
- **Corp** — the megacorporation player. Scores agendas while defending servers with ICE.
- **Click** — an action point. Corp gets 3 per turn, Runner gets 4.
- **Credits** — money used to play and use cards.
- **Agenda** — a Corp card worth points. First to 7 points usually wins.
- **ICE** — the Corp's defenses, placed face down in front of a server. Each new one goes on the outside; adding one costs 1 credit for each ICE already there.
- **Rez** — the Corp paying to flip a face-down card face up so it works.
- **Run** — the Runner attacking a server, passing ICE one piece at a time. Costs 1 click: tap **Run**, then a lit server. Tap Run again to cancel.
- **Jack out** — the Runner giving up a run after passing a piece of ICE. Nothing is accessed.
- **Approach** — the Runner reaching a piece of ICE. If it is face down, the Corp may rez it now (**Rez** / **Don't rez**); ICE the Corp doesn't rez is passed.
- **Encounter** — meeting a face-up piece of ICE. The Runner breaks its subroutines or taps **Let it through**.
- **Subroutine** — a line on a piece of ICE that happens unless the Runner breaks it, like "End the run".
- **Breaker / icebreaker** — a Runner program that gets past ICE. Each kind breaks one kind of ICE: Corroder breaks barriers, Gordian Blade code gates, Ninja sentries.
- **Strength** — how tough a piece of ICE is, and how strong an icebreaker is. A breaker can only break ICE whose strength is no higher than its own; pay to raise it (**+1 strength**). Raised strength lasts for one piece of ICE (Gordian Blade: the rest of the run).
- **Break** — pay an icebreaker's price to cross out one subroutine so it doesn't happen.
- **Barrier / Code gate / Sentry** — the three kinds of ICE. Each icebreaker breaks only one kind, so a break button says which: "Battering Ram: break barrier sub · 2 credits".
- **sub / str** — short for subroutine and strength on run buttons: "break barrier sub", "+1 str". "break up to 2 barrier subs" shows only when 2 or more subroutines are left to break.
- **Access** — the Runner looking at the cards in a server after getting in: HQ shows 1 random card, R&D the top card, Archives every card, a remote server every card in it.
- **Steal** — take an agenda you accessed into your score area (you must). Its points count for the Runner.
- **Trash** — pay a card's trash cost to destroy it (it goes to Archives face up). **Leave it** puts the card back; **Done** puts back a card you can't steal or trash.
- **Advance** — the Corp paying 1 click and 1 credit to put an advancement counter on an installed agenda. Both players see the counters, even on a face-down card.
- **Score** — once an agenda has enough counters, the Corp moves it face up to its score area (no click needed) and gets its points.
- **"When scored" bonus** — some agendas do something extra the moment the Corp scores them (never when the Runner steals them). Hostile Takeover gives the Corp 7 credits and 1 bad publicity; Priority Requisition lets the Corp flip one face-down ICE face up for free (tap a lit ICE, or **Skip**).
- **Bad publicity** — a black mark against the Corp, shown as "Bad publicity 1" by the Corp's credits. For each one, the Runner gets 1 extra credit at the start of every run, usable only during that run (shown as "+1 for this run"); left-over ones disappear when the run ends.
- **Run-only credits** — the Runner's extra credits from bad publicity ("+1 for this run"). They are spent first during the run and vanish when it ends.
- **Identity** — the card that says who you are playing (Haas-Bioroid for the Corp, Kate for the Runner). It sits face up beside your cards all game, gives you a small bonus, and sets your deck's rules: the smallest deck size (45) and how much influence you may spend (15).
- **Influence** — the "budget" for cards from other factions (teams). Each such card costs its dots (1–4); your own faction's cards and neutral cards cost nothing. A deck may spend up to its identity's limit (15). Shown in the deck editor as "Influence 8 / 15".
- **Agenda points** — the points printed on an agenda card. When building a Corp deck they must add up to the right amount for its size (40–44 cards: 18 or 19; 45–49 cards: 20 or 21; each 5 more cards add 2), so a bigger deck needs more agendas. Shown in the deck editor as "Agenda points 21 (needs 20–21)".
- **Ready to play** — a built deck that meets every deck rule, so it can be picked for a game.
- **Standard decks** — the two ready-made 45-card decks (Haas-Bioroid for the Corp, Kate for the Runner) that every game used before Sprint 13. In the Decks screen you can read them but not change them; **Copy to edit** makes your own copy. Online games still use them.
- **Faction** — the "team" a card belongs to (e.g. Haas-Bioroid, Weyland, Jinteki, NBN for the Corp; Shaper, Criminal, Anarch for the Runner), shown under each card's name in the deck editor. Neutral cards belong to no faction. Other factions' cards cost influence; another faction's agendas can't go in a Corp deck at all.
- **Points** — agenda points in a player's score area, shown as "Corp 1 point" / "Runner 2 points". 7 points wins.
- **Net damage** — the Runner discards that many random cards from the Grip to the Heap.
- **Brain damage** — like net damage (random cards from the Grip to the Heap), and the Runner's hand limit also goes down by 1 for each point, for the rest of the game (shown as "Hand max 4").
- **Meat damage** — like net damage: the Runner discards that many random cards from the Grip to the Heap. The Corp can usually only do it while the Runner is tagged.
- **Trace** — a bidding contest started by a card ("Trace 3"). The Corp adds credits to the trace's strength, then the Runner adds credits to his link. If the Corp's total is higher, the card's effect happens; a tie goes to the Runner.
- **Link** — the Runner's number for traces: his identity's printed link plus link cards (Kate has 1; Access to Globalsec adds 1). Shown as "Link 1".
- **Tag / Tagged** — the Corp has found the Runner. Tags stay until he removes one (1 click + 2 credits each). While tagged (shown as "Tagged 1") the Corp may trash his resources (1 click + 2 credits) and use cards like Private Security Force.
- **Flatline** — the Runner takes more damage than cards in the Grip: the Corp wins at once ("Runner flatlined").
- **Server** — a place where the Corp keeps cards. Three are built in (HQ, R&D, Archives); the Corp can open remote servers.
- **HQ** — the Corp's hand (its headquarters).
- **R&D** — the Corp's deck ("research and development").
- **Archives** — the Corp's discard pile. Played operations go there face up; hand-limit discards go face down.
- **Remote server** — a server the Corp opens by installing a card into "New server". Shown as Server 1, Server 2, ... Holds at most one agenda or asset.
- **Install** — put a card from your hand onto the table (1 click). Corp cards go face down into a server; Runner cards go face up into the Runner's area.
- **Operation** — a Corp card you play once for its effect (like Hedge Fund), then it goes to Archives.
- **Event** — a Runner card you play once for its effect (like Diesel), then it goes to the Heap.
- **Program / hardware / resource** — the three kinds of Runner cards that stay installed. Icebreakers are programs.
- **Memory** — room for the Runner's programs. Each program uses some; the Runner has 4.
- **Grip** — the Runner's hand.
- **Stack** — the Runner's deck.
- **Expose** — look at one face-down Corp card on the table (Infiltration). Both players see it; it stays face down and nothing else changes.
- **Recurring credits** — credits that sit on a card and refill at the start of its owner's turn, for one use only (The Toolbox: 2, for icebreakers).
- **Heap** — the Runner's discard pile (always face up).
- **Upgrade** — a Corp card installed in any server (even HQ, R&D or Archives) to help that server.
- **Asset** — a Corp card installed in a remote server that helps the Corp, often with money.
- **Turn** — one player's go. The Corp goes first, then players alternate. You end your turn with the End turn button (leftover clicks are lost).
- **Free draw** — the card the Corp must draw at the start of each of its turns. If its deck is empty then, the Runner wins.
- **Hand limit** — at the end of your turn you may hold at most 5 cards; you pick which extra cards to discard.
- **Discard pile** — where discarded and played cards go. The Runner's (the Heap) is face up; the Corp's (Archives) mixes face-up played cards and face-down discards, which the Runner sees only as card backs.
- **Hot-seat** — two people taking turns on the same device. The board always shows the player whose turn it is at the bottom.
- **Safe area** — the part of a phone screen not covered by the camera notch, status bar or bottom swipe bar. Everything you can tap or read stays inside it.
- **Timing window** — a moment where a player may respond. We skip ones where nobody can do anything.
- **Sprint** — a short round of work ending in a demo you can play.
- **Story** — one feature described from a player's point of view.
- **Acceptance criteria** — the checks that prove a story works.
- **PR (pull request)** — a page on GitHub showing proposed work; you comment there.
